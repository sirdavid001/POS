What remains available

Offline support built around real store interruptions.

QuickPOS does not describe every action as permanently offline. It keeps the records needed for supported daily work on a verified device, queues changes that can be synchronized safely, and clearly reserves security-sensitive or payment-related actions for an internet connection.

01

Checkout from the saved catalog

Search locally saved products, use recorded barcodes, build the cart, select a saved customer, record the payment method, and complete a supported sale without waiting for the connection to return.

02

Keep local stock records useful

Supported sales and management changes update the device's local store snapshot. Product, category, customer, supplier, and inventory records remain available after the device has synchronized online.

03

Queue work instead of losing it

QuickPOS keeps supported offline changes in a per-user sync queue on the device. A temporary network interruption does not require the cashier to recreate the same completed work later.

04

Sync after reconnecting

When the internet returns, the app sends queued work to the authenticated store account, receives the current store state, and refreshes the device snapshot for the next interruption.

A clear boundary

Know which tasks still need the internet.

Reliable offline software should explain its limits before a store depends on it. QuickPOS requires a connection for identity, security, subscription, payment, download, and email actions that should not rely on an old local copy.

Designed to continue offline

  • Supported sales and local receipts
  • Saved product and barcode lookup
  • Cached customers and order history
  • Supported inventory and management queues
  • Locally derived store reporting

Requires a connection

  • First sign-in and initial device sync
  • Activation, renewal, and provider checkout
  • Password resets and staff credential changes
  • Official installer access
  • Email delivery of store statements

Offline entitlement rules

  • Uses the latest verified subscription record
  • Works offline for up to seven days
  • Never extends access past a known paid expiry
  • Refreshes entitlement after reconnecting
  • Keeps each store and user queue separated
Prepare before an outage

Three steps make offline use more dependable.

01

Sign in and synchronize

Connect the installed app before the working day so it has the current product, customer, stock, order, settings, and entitlement records for the store.

02

Continue supported work

If the network drops, use the installed app and watch its connection state. QuickPOS stores supported changes on that device until it can reach the server.

03

Reconnect and review

Restore the connection, allow queued work to synchronize, and confirm the app reports a healthy sync before signing out or clearing local browser or app data.

Frequently asked questions

What stores should know about offline QuickPOS use.

Can QuickPOS record sales without an internet connection?

Yes. After the installed app has completed an online sync, supported sales can be saved to the device queue during a connection interruption and synchronized when the connection returns.

How long can QuickPOS be used offline?

A previously verified QuickPOS subscription can be used offline for up to seven days, but never beyond the paid expiry date already known to the device.

Does every QuickPOS action work offline?

No. First-time setup, subscription and payment checks, password resets, staff credential changes, installer access, and emailed statements require an internet connection.

What happens to inventory after an offline sale?

The installed app applies the supported sale to its local store records and queues the change. When the device reconnects, QuickPOS sends the queued work to the store account and refreshes the local snapshot.

Does offline QuickPOS replace an offline card terminal?

No. QuickPOS records the payment method used for a sale, but it does not replace a bank card terminal or guarantee that a separate payment provider can process a card while offline.
